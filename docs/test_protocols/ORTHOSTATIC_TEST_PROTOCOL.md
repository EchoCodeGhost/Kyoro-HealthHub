# Extended Schellong Test / 10-Minute NASA Lean Test — Home Protocol

> **Deutsche Version:** [ORTHOSTATIC_TEST_PROTOCOL_DE.md](ORTHOSTATIC_TEST_PROTOCOL_DE.md)

**Purpose:** POTS screening when orthostatic intolerance is suspected.
Formal POTS criterion: ΔHR ≥ 30 bpm within 10 minutes of standing (Raj et al., 2021).
This protocol follows the timing schedule of the **10-Minute NASA Lean Test**
(Bateman Horne Center) — the standardized, validated procedure used in
ME/CFS/POTS diagnostics, which goes beyond the 2-3 minutes of a simple
Schellong test (sources at the end of this document).

---

## What you need

| Device | For |
|---|---|
| Chest-strap HR monitor (e.g. Polar H10) | Beat-to-beat HR, gapless |
| Recording app on your phone | Starting HR recording |
| Blood pressure monitor (any) | BP lying down + standing |
| Timer (phone) | 10-minute countdown |
| A wall to lean against | Stability while standing |
| Notebook or app | Recording values |
| A calm companion (**not optional**) | Safety in case of dizziness/syncope, operates devices if needed, records values |

**Device-agnostic:** This protocol works with any blood pressure monitor that
gives you BP+pulse on demand (e.g. Omron X7 Smart) — just trigger each
measurement individually. If your monitor supports **automatic series
measurement** (e.g. Withings BPM Core: "Advanced Mode"/START-3, covers up to
3 of the time points in one press at a 1-minute interval —
[instructions](https://support.withings.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024351713-BPM-Core-Enabling-the-advanced-mode)),
you can use that to save button presses — just verify first whether your
device stores/exports the individual readings with their own timestamp, or
only the average (if only the average, note the readings from the live
display instead of relying on the export).

**Why a chest strap instead of a cuff for pulse:** Cuff-based blood
pressure monitors often measure pulse inaccurately during strong, fast
fluctuations — exactly what happens when standing up. A chest strap,
finger pulse oximeter, or smartwatch/pulse app is more reliable for the
pulse reading itself; the cuff is only needed for the blood pressure
values.

## Preparation

- **Time of day:** Morning, 30-60 min after waking — NOT immediately after waking
  (the cortisol peak skews the baseline). Ideal: 09:00-11:00.
- **No caffeine** in the 2h before
- **No exercise** in the 4h before
- **Well hydrated:** drink 500 ml of water about 30 min before the test
  (hypovolemia is a common POTS cause; hydration doesn't reduce false positives,
  but it improves reproducibility)
- **Warm environment:** cold causes vasoconstriction and skews the HR response
- **No food** for 2h before (postprandial hypotension can produce a POTS-like pattern)
- **No compression stockings** — they alter the orthostatic response and
  skew the result
- **Medications/substances:** Some medications and substances (e.g. blood
  pressure medication, stimulants including caffeine, tricyclic
  antidepressants) alter the values. Whether pausing a medication before
  the test makes sense and is possible should be clarified with a doctor
  beforehand — don't pause prescription medication on your own

## Procedure

### Phase 1: Lying down (10 minutes)

1. Put on the HR monitor, start recording
2. Lie flat (no pillow under the head), legs extended
3. **Lie still for 10 minutes** — don't talk, don't use your phone
4. At minute 9-10: measure blood pressure (lying down)
5. Note the HR values

**Record: HR lying down (average of the last 2 min), BP lying down**

### Phase 2: Standing up and standing (10 minutes)

6. Stand up calmly — **not quickly, don't push off with your arms**
7. Lean against the wall — **heels about 6-8 inches (15-20 cm) from the
   wall**, shoulder blades touching the wall (official NASA Lean Test
   posture, Bateman Horne Center). Alternative: leaning fully against the
   wall (head, back, heels touching) dampens leg muscle contractions even
   more reliably, but isn't the posture the published NASA Lean Test
   reference values were collected with — for comparability with the
   literature, use the 6-8 inch variant.
8. Start the timer

**Measure and record:**

| Time point | HR | BP | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standing 0 min (immediately) | | | |
| Standing 1 min | | | |
| Standing 2 min | | | |
| Standing 3 min | | | |
| Standing 5 min | | | |
| Standing 7 min | | | |
| Standing 10 min | | | |

**Document symptoms:** dizziness, palpitations, visual disturbances,
pallor, nausea, weakness, headache — and at what time point.

### Phase 3: Lying down (recovery)

9. Lie back down
10. Note HR after 2 min — how quickly does HR recover?

## Interpretation

### POTS criterion (Raj et al., 2021)
**ΔHR ≥ 30 bpm** within 10 minutes of standing **without orthostatic hypotension**

### Orthostatic hypotension
**BP drop ≥ 20 mmHg systolic or ≥ 10 mmHg diastolic** within 3 min of standing
(this would be a different diagnosis than POTS)

### Gray zones
- ΔHR 20-29 bpm: orthostatic intolerance without formal POTS — clinically relevant
- HR > 120 bpm while standing: POTS-compatible even without ΔHR ≥ 30 (if symptomatic)

## Why a 2-minute quick test often falls short

Many short protocols only test 2-3 minutes of standing. This can be a false negative:

- Many affected people need 5-10 minutes for the maximal HR rise
- Arms not braced against a wall: muscle contractions dampen the HR rise
- Time of day and hydration substantially affect the result (see Preparation above)
- Per the Bateman Horne Center, **at least 5 minutes** of standing are needed
  to pick up an orthostatic intolerance pattern in most patients — the full
  10 minutes of this protocol pick up more

**Test multiple times:** POTS severity varies with time of day, hydration, hormonal
status, sleep, and warmth. At least 3 tests on different days for a reliable result.

---

## For your cardiologist / neurologist

Bring these results:
- Raw HR data (RR-interval time series, if recorded via a chest strap)
- Blood pressure values (lying + standing, multiple time points)
- Symptom timestamps
- Results from multiple home tests, if available

A clinical tilt-table test (70° tilt, 45 minutes) is the gold standard —
it can be requested after a positive home Schellong test.

---

## Safety note

Dizziness up to fainting (syncope) can occur during the test. **As a
rule, only do this test with a companion present** — they can operate
devices if needed, record values, and intervene in an emergency, while
you stand calmly and relaxed against the wall.

For severe dizziness, visual impairment, pallor, or feeling faint:
**lie down immediately**, elevate the legs. Stop the test and only resume after
full recovery (>30 min).

**For ME/CFS:** The test itself can trigger a PEM crash — deliberately
plan recovery time afterward instead of scheduling further appointments
or exertion right after.
